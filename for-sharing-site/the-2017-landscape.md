# The 2017 landscape

Much of this likely still applies. As the landscape stood at LIP discovery in 2017:

## Three species-specific systems, no commonality

- **CTS** for cattle (RPA, ~19m transactions a year, old and disjointed)
- **ARAMS** for sheep (Capita, ~700k transactions a year)
- **eAML2** for pigs (AHDB, ~175k — regarded as the exemplar, built iteratively and user-led)

Around 180,000 users and £16m a year to run, with costs split roughly half IT, half service operations.

## The system fought its own users

- **Six different systems were needed to issue one cattle passport.** Staff workarounds everywhere; downstream systems (notably RADAR) degraded through underinvestment; master data not updated when errors were found.
- **The incentive structure poisons data quality.** Compliance is driven by fear of losing Basic Payment Scheme money, so keepers don't report their own mistakes.
- **The 2001 foot-and-mouth outbreak (~£2bn) is the canonical failure case** — and controls were judged only marginally better in 2017.

## The vision could be written today

The programme's vision was:

> "A single multi-species traceability service to identify and track livestock destined for the food chain and integrate traceability data across the UK and its devolved administrations."

That sentence could be written today. Asking why it wasn't achieved is a fast route into the politics of this space.

A late addition to LIP's guiding principles, mid-2017:

> "Ensure we are prepared on Day 1 to continue to trade with the EU… whatever the final settlement is."

Day-1 readiness language is not new to this domain.

## The policy ambitions catalogue

LIP catalogued around 25 policy ambitions — paperless movement documents, removing cattle passports, whole-movement reporting, bovine EID, real-time market reporting, dealer identification, food chain information collection, carcase feedback to producers and more — and mapped which needed legislation.

Checking that catalogue against what actually shipped since 2017 would produce a very sharp gap analysis. See [Recommendations](recommendations).
