# Inland traceability — lessons from two previous Defra programmes

Dug Falby worked as a service designer on the Livestock Information Programme (LIP, 2016–18) and on the NI Trade / Digital Assistance Scheme work (2021). He made extensive notes during his time on both programmes. This site is a synthesis of those notes, prepared as a handover for the team working in the live environment.

## Why history matters here

This is at least the third run at the same underlying problem: **the UK cannot quickly and credibly answer "where are the animals, and where have they been?"** — and every trade settlement with the EU eventually forces the question.

LIP tried to fix the domestic data layer. The Trade project tried to fix the certification layer that compensates for a weak data layer. The EU reset now removes checks at the border, which pushes the burden of proof back onto exactly the inland systems LIP was created to modernise. The two earlier programmes are, in effect, the as-is research for today's one.

## A note on vintage

Findings are 5–9 years old. Systems have changed: the Livestock Information Service now exists for sheep in England, and devolved systems have evolved. Treat everything here as hypothesis, not fact.
