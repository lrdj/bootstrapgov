# The 2021 layer: certification and NI

The 2021 work sat one layer up from traceability: when the domestic data layer can't prove provenance and health, the certification layer has to compensate — at real cost.

## The Digital Assistance Scheme

DAS existed to make GB→NI agri-food movement survivable once grace periods ended: an end-to-end digital flow from provenance data capture → EHC Online → e-certification by official vets → the EU's TRACES NT → automatic CHED creation. The strategic case for it is a concise education in why certification cost and complexity threaten supply chains.

## The hard dependency was on the EU side

The critical dependency was the EU-side API into TRACES NT — an external cooperation risk, not a build risk. **Watch for the same shape of dependency in the reset work.**

## What certifier research found

Research with certifying vets on EHC Online / eTrade found:

- status language that made sense to the system but not to vets ("approved" should say "certified")
- no search by EHC number or exporter
- no edit route for the fields that genuinely change late — vehicle, seal number, weights, ports
- a need to see work-in-progress rather than archives

Vet certification capacity and usability was the bottleneck.

## Reusable process thinking

The 2021 team drafted around 45 process maps of GB↔NI flows by commodity (livestock, products of animal origin, equine, fish, plants, vet meds and more) — probably the single most reusable artefact set for a team mapping cross-border journeys today, even if details have moved on.

The domain also runs on acronyms; a maintained glossary was essential kit. Recommend issuing one to every new joiner.
