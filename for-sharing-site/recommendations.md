# Recommendations

Five suggested first moves, drawn from the synthesis. Each is framed as an offer, not a plan: what it is, why it pays, and where to start. Together they share one intent — **start this programme knowing what the last two learned, rather than paying to learn it again.**

## 1. Gap-analyse the 2017 policy ambitions against 2026 reality

`quick win`

LIP catalogued around 25 policy ambitions — paperless movement documents, removing cattle passports, whole-movement reporting, bovine EID, carcase feedback and more — and mapped which needed legislation.

**Why:** working out what shipped (Livestock Information Service, bovine EID status, cattle passports?), what didn't, and why, yields informed questions rather than naive ones. The "why not" answers are a fast route into the politics of this space.

**First step:** list the ambitions, RAG them against current reality, and book an hour with someone who worked on the Livestock Information Service to close the gaps.

**Evidence:** [The 2017 landscape](the-2017-landscape)

## 2. Retest the personas and pain points — don't rediscover them

`quick win` · `shapes the research plan`

The 2017 personas (the sheep farmer drowning in ARAMS errors, the multi-species keeper, the mart, the high-tech abattoir, the enforcement officer) and the nine recurring research themes remain strong hypotheses.

**Why:** framing early research as "what has changed since these findings?" is faster, and more credible with participants who are tired of being asked the same questions by successive programmes.

**First step:** build the first discussion guides around the nine themes as hypotheses to confirm, adjust or retire.

**Evidence:** [What research found](research-findings)

## 3. Reuse the process thinking

`needs archive access`

Value stream maps and as-is blueprints exist from LIP; around 45 commodity-level GB↔NI flow maps exist from 2021; the DAS end-to-end journey (provenance capture → certification → TRACES NT) was mapped.

**Why:** straw-man artefacts accelerate early workshops. People correct a wrong map far faster than they populate a blank one.

**First step:** redraw two or three of the most relevant flows as deliberately provisional workshop straw-men, clearly dated so nobody mistakes them for current state.

**Evidence:** [The 2021 layer](certification-and-ni)

## 4. Chase the people trail

`quick win` · `compounds over time`

A stakeholder mapping was done in 2017–18 covering Defra, agencies, devolved administrations, industry bodies and suppliers.

**Why:** some of these people (or their successors in role) are still in the system, and a single conversation with the right one recovers months of institutional memory.

**First step:** cross-check the list against current org charts; prioritise the roles over the individuals; book the first three conversations.

**Evidence:** [Stakeholders](stakeholders)

## 5. Design for keeper value, not enforcement

`design principle` · `needs policy input`

The 2017 research found the incentive structure poisons data quality: compliance driven by fear means keepers don't report their own mistakes. The distributed ledger concept inverted this — make good record-keeping valuable to the keeper, and derive compliance from it.

**Why:** every previous attempt that led with enforcement bought resentment and bad data. Whatever the 2026 mechanism turns out to be, the principle — compliance as a by-product of something the keeper already wants to do — is the one lever the earlier programmes identified but never pulled.

**First step:** pressure-test the ledger ecosystem loops against current policy and technology; even where the mechanism fails, each loop is a checklist question for any proposed design ("what does the keeper get?").

**Evidence:** [Two ideas worth re-examining](ledger-concept)

## Cautions

> **Vintage.** Findings are 5–9 years old. Systems have changed — the Livestock Information Service now exists for sheep in England; devolved systems have evolved. Treat everything as hypothesis, not fact.
>
> **Survivorship.** Both source programmes struggled. Notes from the time document intent far better than they document outcomes — read confident roadmaps in that light.
